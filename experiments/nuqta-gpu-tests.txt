Per-page time: the median is 3.49 s for a full page and 0.79 s for line mode, and the 10th–90th percentile range is 2.2–6.6 s against 0.54–1.3 s. Segmentation is under 0.1 s of that.
Batch: all 70 pages in one call take 40.9 s for full pages and 55.5 s for read_lines. read_lines batches lines one page at a time, so on a batch it loses. Batching the crops of all pages in a single read_many call should fix that; it could be a follow-up.
Is the 1.7-point gap stable? Mostly, and it depends on the print. The median gap is 0.9 points. 38 pages are within 2 points, line mode is worse by more than 2 points on 27 and better on 2.
On clean modern print it is close to zero: UN is +0.1 points and finepdfs −0.8.
Turath is −1.5. Part of that is Quran quotations: read alone, a line sometimes switches to Uthmani spelling (ٱ instead of ا), which the page doesn't print.
VanDyck, a 19th-century Bible print, is −4.5. Segmentation there is right (every printed line is boxed); the model simply misreads more worn letters when it sees one line without the rest of the page.
Tashkeel consistency: on fully vowelled pages, 25 of 554 lines (4.5%) came back with no tashkeel at all, while the full-page reading lost the marks on only 1 of 30 such pages. Each crop decides for itself whether the text is vowelled. On VanDyck, line mode actually copies marks better (73.7% against 67.4%).
Mixed Arabic/Latin/digit pages: I only found 3 in our test sets that pass a strict filter: two UN pages and one Kuwaiti legal page. Both modes read them equally well: Latin words 96–100%, digits 100%, Arabic 98–100%.

For the word-box question the bigger number is this: 34% of all 1,620 detected lines mix Arabic with digits or Latin, mostly verse numbers, footnote marks and dates. VanDyck has them on 73% of lines and turath on 28%. So the single-word RTL reversal you found in OCRmyPDF would affect about a third of lines on typical book pages, which makes word-level boxes worth doing next.
